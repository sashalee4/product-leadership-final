# Meridian Team Charter

## Purpose

We jointly build Meridian into the fastest and most trusted way for field superintendents and foremen to document work, communicate progress, and protect themselves from disputes—without adding administrative burden.

Our success is measured by behavior and outcomes:

- Field teams choose Meridian over phone cameras, texts, and WhatsApp.
- Weekly field activity increases across active projects.
- Documentation and reporting time decreases.
- Project records become reliable enough to support communication, accountability, and dispute protection.
- Enterprise value is supported without undermining the field experience or long-term product foundation.

## What We Own

### Foundations

The Foundations owner owns:

- The outcomes, scope, sequencing, and dependencies of the Foundations initiative.
- Connecting foundational investments to field adoption, enterprise value, reliability, or delivery speed.
- Identifying technical risks, trade-offs, and decisions requiring leadership attention.

The Engineering technical owner owns:

- Architecture and implementation approach.
- Technical feasibility, estimates, quality, scalability, security, and maintainability.
- Technical recommendations and risks associated with roadmap choices.

### Field Adoption and Mobile Experience

PM 1 owns:

- Field-user problems and workflows.
- Mobile experience and job-site usability.
- Adoption, weekly field activity, and documentation-time outcomes.
- Field validation and the field adoption roadmap.

### Enterprise

PM 2 owns:

- Enterprise customer problems and requirements.
- Commercial context, renewal risks, and enterprise outcomes.
- The enterprise roadmap and communication with enterprise stakeholders.

PM 2 may gather and recommend enterprise commitments but may not promise delivery without the required product, engineering, and commercial approvals.

### Shared Responsibilities

We jointly own:

- Maintaining one integrated roadmap.
- Making product, technical, capacity, and commercial trade-offs explicit.
- Delivering reliable, usable experiences in real job-site conditions.
- Communicating roadmap decisions and consequences to stakeholders.
- Measuring whether our work creates field, enterprise, and business value.

Product leadership owns portfolio priority and customer-value trade-offs. Engineering leadership owns technical feasibility, quality, and operational risk.

## Out of Scope

We do not:

- Automatically prioritize an enterprise request because it is tied to a renewal.
- Allow Sales or another stakeholder to unilaterally reprioritize the roadmap.
- Move Engineering capacity informally or through private agreements.
- Treat PMs as substitutes for technical ownership or Engineers as substitutes for product ownership.
- Commit to work before identifying capacity, timing, dependencies, and opportunity cost.
- Build one-off customer functionality without understanding its strategic value and long-term ownership.
- Treat Foundations as an open-ended platform effort disconnected from measurable product or business outcomes.
- Optimize for feature volume, logins, or content creation without evidence of improved outcomes.

## Protected Field Bet

The field adoption bet receives a planned Engineering capacity allocation for the quarter.

It may be reduced or displaced only through a documented portfolio decision that identifies:

- The commercial rationale and evidence.
- The field outcome at risk.
- The Foundations work affected.
- The work being delayed, stopped, or descoped.
- The expected duration of the trade-off.
- The accountable approver.
- The date the decision will be revisited.

An enterprise request does not create automatic priority. It must be evaluated based on strategic fit, customer and business impact, confidence in the outcome, technical cost, and opportunity cost to the field bet.

## Joint Decisions Required Before Action

The accountable Product and Engineering owners must make a joint call before:

- Reallocating Engineering capacity from field adoption to enterprise work.
- Accepting an enterprise feature as a renewal commitment.
- Changing the scope, sequencing, or staffing of Foundations.
- Pausing or materially delaying a field adoption bet.
- Making significant architectural, platform, data, or API changes.
- Accepting a material trade-off in reliability, security, offline capability, mobile performance, or maintainability.
- Committing to work that materially affects another team’s capacity or systems.

A joint call requires input from the relevant owners; it does not require unanimous agreement from every stakeholder.

## How We Decide

| Decision | Accountable owner |
|---|---|
| Field adoption and mobile roadmap | PM 1 |
| Enterprise roadmap and requirements | PM 2 |
| Foundations outcomes and sequencing | Foundations owner |
| Architecture and implementation approach | Engineering technical owner |
| Product strategy and portfolio priorities | Product leader |
| Engineering feasibility, quality, and operational risk | Engineering leader |
| Engineering capacity allocation | Product leader and Engineering leader jointly |
| External renewal commitments | Product leader and designated commercial leader |
| Reallocating committed capacity | Product leader, with Engineering leader input and documented approval |

When Product and Engineering leaders disagree about reallocating committed capacity, the decision escalates to the designated executive sponsor. Product leadership owns the portfolio priority decision; Engineering leadership owns the technical risk assessment.

## Escalation Path

1. **Resolve directly within one business day.**  
   PM 1, PM 2, the Foundations owner, and the relevant Engineering lead identify the conflict, options, capacity implications, and recommendation.

2. **Escalate to Product and Engineering leadership within two business days if unresolved.**  
   The escalation must identify the customer or field outcome at stake, the commercial evidence, the technical consequences, and the work that would be delayed.

3. **Reach a leadership decision within three business days.**  
   The decision must state what changes, what is protected, what is deferred, who approved it, and when it will be revisited.

4. **Escalate to the executive sponsor within one additional business day when necessary.**  
   This applies to major renewal risk, company-level commitments, significant investment, or unresolved disagreement between Product and Engineering leadership.

While a conflict is unresolved, no irreversible capacity shift or external commitment may be made. Reversible discovery and technical investigation may continue.

## Decision Record

Material decisions are recorded with:

- Decision
- Accountable owner
- Date
- Outcome being optimized
- Options considered
- Trade-offs accepted
- Risks and follow-up actions
- Revisit date, if applicable

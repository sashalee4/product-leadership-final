# Meridian Trade-off Memo

## Decision

This quarter, we are optimizing for one outcome: making Meridian the fastest and most trusted way for field teams to document work and communicate progress, without adding administrative burden.

## What we chose to sequence first

I chose to sequence reliable field capture first because poor connectivity and an ill-fitting mobile workflow are the highest-cost barriers to adoption. Removing those barriers directly supports KR1 and KR3: more weekly active field users and more projects with activity at least five days per week. In WSJF terms, these bets have high cost of delay because every week they remain unresolved, field teams continue defaulting to phone cameras, texts, and WhatsApp.

I then prioritized reducing the daily log to essential information because speed is necessary for the new workflow to become a habit. This directly supports KR2 by targeting the 50% reduction in reporting and documentation time.

## What we pushed out

I pushed out deeper project-office workflows and richer progress communication because they depend on field adoption first. If foremen are not consistently creating timely, trusted field records, downstream PM workflows will have limited value and may add complexity before the core behavior is established.

I also pushed out proactive dispute prevention, intelligent summaries, and broader automation because their value depends on having a reliable volume of field activity and project evidence. These are strategically important, but their cost of delay is lower than fixing the adoption and administrative-burden problems that block the strategy today.

## What we cut entirely

I cut broad PM web-app expansion and feature parity with the foreman experience entirely for this quarter because they do not address the primary constraint: getting field teams to choose Meridian over informal tools. They would consume capacity without directly moving the three quarterly KRs.

I also cut optimizing for logins, content creation, or other activity metrics as standalone goals. They are weak proxies for success and could encourage behavior that increases product usage without reducing reporting time, improving field consistency, or creating a trusted project record.

## Trade-off

The deliberate trade-off is depth over breadth. We are concentrating resources on the smallest set of bets that can change field behavior now, while deferring capabilities that become more valuable once Meridian is used consistently in the field.
